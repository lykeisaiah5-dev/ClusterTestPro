Repair or replace?

Repairing your original module may be an option

For eligible failures, repairing the module already in your vehicle can be an alternative to sourcing a replacement. Whether it fits your situation depends on the vehicle, the module and the fault.

Repairing your original module

  • You send the module from your vehicle and receive the same unit back.
  • Only available for the part numbers and failures listed on each service page.
  • Programming or setup needs vary by vehicle and module. Each service page states what applies. [Placeholder: confirm per service]
  • The vehicle is without the module while it is in transit and being repaired.

Replacing the module

  • A different unit — new, used or remanufactured — is sourced and installed.
  • Replacement modules may need programming, coding or setup for your vehicle.
  • Availability and part-number matching depend on the supplier.
  • May be the better route if the failure isn't one we list, or the module has physical damage.

Not sure which applies? Send us your part number, fault codes and symptoms and we'll tell you whether a listed repair service fits.

Ask a Question
How do I know whether my module is eligible?

Find the part number printed on your module's label and match it to the part numbers listed on a repair service page. The vehicle finder helps narrow the list, but the part number is what confirms eligibility. Some faults also need to match the symptoms and codes the service covers.

If your part number isn't listed, or you're unsure, send us your details before ordering.

Am I purchasing a repair or a replacement?

A repair. Every service on this site is a mail-in repair of your own module. We do not ship a module to you from inventory — you send us yours, and we return the same unit after the covered repair.

What should I send?

It depends on the service. Each service page states whether to send the electronic controller on its own or a complete assembly. Follow the “What to send” section for the service you purchase.

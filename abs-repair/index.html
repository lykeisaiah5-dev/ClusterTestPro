Repair versus replacement

When repairing the original module makes sense

Repair is an option for eligible failures — not every failure. Here is how the two paths differ in practice.

Repairing your original module

  • May be an option when the failure falls within a covered repair scope for your module's part number.
  • The unit returned to you is the module that was installed in your vehicle. Its stored configuration is normally unchanged, though some vehicles still require a procedure after reinstallation — each service page says what to expect.
  • Priced per service. Compare the listed price against a replacement quote for your specific vehicle rather than assuming a saving.
  • Requires removing and shipping the module, so allow for bench turnaround plus carrier transit in both directions.

Replacing the module

  • The right path when the failure is outside a repairable scope, or when the module is physically damaged, water-damaged, or has been opened before.
  • New or used replacements may need programming or coding to your vehicle. Requirements vary by manufacturer and model year.
  • Availability and cost vary widely with the vehicle's age and the module's supply.
  • We do not sell replacement modules. If yours is not repairable, we tell you and return it under our unrepairable-module policy.

Whether repair is the right call depends on the specific failure. If you are unsure, send us your fault codes and symptoms before purchasing.

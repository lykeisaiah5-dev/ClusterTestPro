How the mail-in process works

You purchase a repair service, send us your module, and receive the same module back after the covered repair and applicable testing. Nothing is shipped from inventory.

The four steps

  1. Step 1

    Find and purchase the correct repair service

    Search by the part number printed on your module label, or narrow by vehicle. Open the service page and confirm three things: your part number is listed, your symptoms fall within the covered scope, and none of the exclusions apply. Then check out through our store. Each service is priced per module.

    Find My Repair

  2. Step 2

    Package and send your module using the supplied instructions

    The service page states exactly what to send — the control module alone, or the complete pump and module assembly. Pack it in a rigid box with cushioning on all sides, put your order number inside, and ship with tracking to the address on the Shipping Instructions page.

  3. Step 3

    We inspect and perform the covered repair and applicable testing

    On arrival the module is checked in against your order and inspected. If it is within the covered scope, we carry out the repair described on the service page.

    If inspection finds a condition outside the covered scope — water damage, physical damage, a prior repair attempt — we contact you before doing further work. [COMMUNICATION POLICY — how and when customers are updated]

  4. Step 4

    We return your module with the relevant service information

    The same module you sent is shipped back to you. [RETURN SHIPPING] Reinstall it following the programming and calibration note on the service page for your module.

Compatibility checklist

Work through this before purchasing. A vehicle match alone is not enough — the same model can carry more than one module version.

  • Locate the label on the module. It is on the controller housing; on some assemblies there is a second label on the hydraulic block. Photograph it in good light.
  • Match the part number exactly. Search the number on this site. Suffix letters and revision digits matter — a different suffix can mean a different circuit board.
  • Compare your symptoms and codes to the covered scope. Each service lists what it addresses and its limitations. Codes pointing to wheel-speed sensors, wiring, or hydraulics are usually not module faults.
  • Read the exclusions. Water intrusion, corrosion, physical damage, and previous repair attempts are typically excluded or need assessment first.
  • Check what to send. Some services accept the controller only; others require the complete assembly.
  • Unsure about any item? Send us the details before you order. It costs nothing and avoids shipping a module we cannot help with.
